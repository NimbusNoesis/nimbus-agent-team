St. Augustine of Hippo is one of the most influential theologians in the history of Christianity. His writings on grace, the Trinity, and the Christian life have shaped both Eastern and Western theology for over fifteen centuries.

## Life

Born in Thagaste, North Africa, in 354, Augustine spent his youth in intellectual searching and moral struggle. After years as a follower of Manichaeism and a teacher of rhetoric in Carthage, Rome, and Milan, he was converted to Christianity through the influence of his mother, St. Monica, and the preaching of St. Ambrose, Bishop of Milan.

Baptized at Easter 387, Augustine returned to North Africa, was ordained priest, and became Bishop of Hippo in 396. He served the Church tirelessly until his death in 430, as the Vandals besieged his city.

## Writings

- **Confessions** — his spiritual autobiography, one of the most beloved books in Christian literature
- **The City of God** — a monumental work of Christian philosophy and theology
- **On the Trinity** — a systematic exploration of Trinitarian doctrine
- **Homilies, letters, and treatises** numbering in the hundreds

## Feast Day

**August 28** in the Western calendar.
