St. Gregory the Great served as Pope of Rome from 590 to 604 and is venerated as a saint in both the Eastern and Western Churches. He is one of the four great Latin Doctors of the Church, alongside Sts. Ambrose, Augustine, and Jerome.

## His Life

Born into a Roman senatorial family around 540, Gregory initially pursued a career in public service, rising to the office of Prefect of Rome. After his father's death, he converted the family estate on the Caelian Hill into a monastery dedicated to St. Andrew and embraced the monastic life.

Called from the monastery to serve as papal legate to Constantinople, and later elected Pope against his own wishes, Gregory devoted himself to the care of the poor, the reform of the clergy, the evangelization of the nations (most famously sending St. Augustine of Canterbury to England), and the enrichment of the Church's worship.

## His Liturgical Legacy

St. Gregory is best known for his reforms of the Roman liturgy — organizing and codifying the prayers, chants, and ceremonies of the Mass into the form that would serve the Western Church for over a millennium. The "Gregorian" chant tradition bears his name, and the classical Roman Rite is often called the "Liturgy of St. Gregory" in his honor.

It is this ancient liturgical heritage — the Mass of St. Gregory — that our parish celebrates today, restored to its Orthodox context within the Antiochian Archdiocese.

## His Writings

Gregory was a prolific author whose works shaped Western Christian thought for centuries:

- **Pastoral Rule** (*Regula Pastoralis*) — a guide for bishops that became the standard manual of pastoral care
- **Dialogues** — accounts of the lives and miracles of Italian saints
- **Morals on the Book of Job** (*Moralia in Job*) — an extensive commentary blending exegesis with moral and mystical theology
- **Homilies on the Gospels and on Ezekiel**

## Feast Day

St. Gregory the Great is commemorated on **March 12** in the Western calendar.
