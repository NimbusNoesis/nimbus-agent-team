## What Is Western Rite Orthodoxy?

Most people associate Orthodox Christianity with the Byzantine liturgical tradition — the Divine Liturgy of St. John Chrysostom, iconography, and chanting in the Eastern style. But the Orthodox Church has always encompassed a diversity of liturgical expressions.

Before the Great Schism of 1054, the Western Church — centered in Rome — was fully Orthodox in faith while celebrating its own distinct liturgical traditions. The Mass of St. Gregory the Great, the Daily Office, and the Western calendar of saints all developed within the undivided Church.

## The Restoration

In the twentieth century, the Antiochian Orthodox Christian Archdiocese formally restored these ancient Western forms for use in Orthodox parishes. The Western Rite Vicariate, established under the Archdiocese, provides oversight and resources for parishes celebrating the Liturgy of St. Gregory (based on the historic Roman Rite) and the Liturgy of St. Tikhon (drawing from Anglican liturgical heritage).

## Our Worship

At St. Gregory's, we celebrate the **Mass (Divine Liturgy) according to the Rite of St. Gregory the Great** — the ancient form of worship used by the Western Church for over a millennium, now restored to its Orthodox context. The service is entirely in English.

We also observe the **Daily Office** (the Liturgy of the Hours), including Matins, Vespers, and Compline, following the ancient Western monastic pattern.
